## Candidate portal implications
The form is useful evidence for a candidate **employer job request → agency verification → worker-facing job-description** workflow, not an unchanged public-job-post template.

### Candidate structured job-order fields
1. Employer/account and authorized contact; agency validation status.
2. Worksite location with separate internal and worker-visible/public precision rules.
3. Job title, duties, employment status, anticipated start, hours/day, days/schedule, live-in/out, meals/lodging, and work-condition/safety information.
4. Compensation as a versioned range/rate and pay basis, rather than a single unconstrained text field.
5. Employer attestations, agency review/referral status, and immutable published/referral snapshots.

### Required review controls before making it an employer portal page
- A public or worker-visible job page must not expose a household’s exact street address by default. Use least-precise location suitable for matching; reveal exact worksite only under an approved, need-to-know workflow.
- A job request is not automatically publishable/referrable. Require employer/contact/job-order verification, agency review, lawful-content moderation, and a tracked current-opening state.
- If a job is advertised in a covered NYC context, pay-transparency rules require counsel-approved handling of a good-faith minimum/maximum hourly or annual range; this form’s single hourly-rate/minimum-wage fields are not by themselves sufficient for that use.
- Do not allow discriminatory, criminal-history, salary-history, credit-history, or proxy-based conditions in job descriptions, employer free text, matching, or publication; route exceptions to the approved review process.
- Keep published/referred job snapshots immutable. Corrections, closure, cancellation, changed terms, or new openings create attributable new versions/events rather than modifying historical referral evidence.
