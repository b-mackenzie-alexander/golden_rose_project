# Golden Rose — New York requirements research matrix

**Scope / caution.** Desk research from primary New York State/NYC legislative and agency sources, checked **2026-07-28**. This is not legal advice or a validated software requirement. Every source-backed result and product control below is a **suggested candidate** pending current documentation or advice from qualified legal counsel, applicability review, and client acceptance. The research does not conclude that the portal, rather than the household client, is the statutory employer or responsible person.

## Suggested findings from public-source research

| Brief proposition | Source-backed result |
|---|---|
| GBL §181(4) has mandatory fee-receipt language | **Suggested from public-source research; counsel validation required.** The source text appears to require the receipt to identify the applicant; agency name/address; date/amount of each fee, deposit, consideration or payment; purpose; and receiving person’s signature. Except for theatrical work it also appears to require, immediately above signature, a boxed bold-capitals statement: **“An employment agency may not charge you, the job applicant, a fee before referring you to a job that you accept. If you pay a fee before accepting a job or pay a fee that otherwise violates the law, you may demand a refund, which shall be repaid within seven days”.** Out-of-state domestic/household recruits may additionally require a copy of §184. [GBL §181](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/181) §4. |
| Household-worker fee caps are percentage based | **Suggested from public-source research; classification and counsel validation required.** The source text appears to classify “domestics” and household employees as **Class A** and describes applicant maximums based on first full month’s pay: 10% (no meals/lodging), 12% (one meal/working day), 14% (two meals/working day), 18% (three meals and lodging/working day); for a mutually understood job under one month, the same percentage of wages actually paid. [GBL §185](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/185) §§4–5. No percentage, classification, or fee behavior may be implemented until counsel confirms current applicability. |
| Payment timing/schedules | **Suggested from public-source research; counsel validation required.** The source text appears to permit applicant consideration only under a written contract after a referral results in employment and appears to prohibit applicant registration fees, deposits, or advance fees. It also describes installment timing and a separate interstate-domestic schedule. [GBL §185](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/185) §§1–3; [GBL §184](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/184) §4(b)(6). No schedule or payment control may be implemented from this summary alone. |
| Three-year registers | **Suggested from public-source research; counsel validation required.** The source text appears to describe three-year retention for specified agency registers, records, reference investigations, contracts, terms/conditions, receipts, and applicant documents, with different trigger language by record type. [GBL §179](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/179); [GBL §181](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/181) §5. No retention period or lock may be implemented until counsel confirms record scope, trigger, storage, and disposal requirements. |
| Applicant payments | **Suggested from public-source research; counsel validation required.** The source text appears to prohibit registration fees, deposits, and advance fees and to condition applicant fees and refunds on specified events and limits. [GBL §§185–186](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/185). No fee or refund behavior may be implemented from this summary alone. |

## High-priority counsel questions before launch

1. Confirm Golden Rose’s Article 11/NYC DCWP licensing status, entity/premises/online operations, required bond/posting, and whether every pricing path is an agency “fee.”
2. Approve a **Class A** applicant-fee schedule and contract/receipt templates. Product should not collect an applicant deposit, registration charge, advance fee, or fee before an accepted resulting placement.
3. Decide whether Golden Rose is only agency, a payroll/recordkeeping vendor, temporary-help firm, or possible joint employer; this determines wage/payroll/leave/tax responsibilities.
4. Approve a domestic-work role taxonomy: household employee vs independent contractor; live-in; private nursing/home care; casual/relative exclusions; meal/lodging treatment; wage-order/local-law overlays.
5. Confirm whether interstate recruiting is in scope. If yes, implement §184 as a separate, counsel-approved workflow before use.
6. Complete privacy/security design review: private-information inventory; processor/vendor contracts; retention and deletion; breach roles/notice playbook; biometric/health-document handling; any AI/AEDT assessment.

## Software-validation path

1. Obtain current legal documentation or advice from qualified counsel that identifies the authoritative source/version, applicability, required wording or calculation, effective date, exceptions, and responsible client owner.
2. Record client acceptance and convert only the approved portion into a versioned requirement and acceptance tests.
3. Keep legal content, rates, schedules, receipts, agreements, and retention behavior configurable and disabled until the affected release gate is approved.
4. Revalidate when the source, business model, jurisdiction, workflow, or counsel guidance changes.

Until those steps are complete, this document supports discovery and interface design only; it does not validate legal behavior in the software.
