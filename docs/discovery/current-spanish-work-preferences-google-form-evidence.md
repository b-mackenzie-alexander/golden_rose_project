## Critical audience correction
Although it was described as a Google form sent to Spanish-speaking “clients,” its title, instructions, and questions strongly indicate that it is **worker/applicant-facing**, not an employer/client intake form.

Evidence:
- Title: **Golden Rose Employment Agency – Perfil de Preferencias Laborales** (*Work Preferences Profile*).
- Intro: “Queremos encontrar el trabajo ideal para ti...” (*We want to find the ideal job for you...*).
- Questions ask what work the respondent wants or does not want, desired pay, availability, commute, and work preferences.
- The final page discusses measurements such as worker supply, willingness to live in, worker-preference compatibility, and the “zona[s] con mayor oferta de trabajadoras” (*areas with the greatest worker supply*).

**Working classification:** Current Spanish-language worker preference / matching-profile intake. Confirm actual audience, distribution, respondent role, and Google account/response handling with Golden Rose.

## Completion-state ambiguity
The PDF visually shows checkmarks next to all/most options for some later questions (notably geographic areas, job priorities, and some continuation options), while earlier option sets display empty boxes. The PDF does not establish whether these are selected answers, form defaults, or a rendering/export artifact. Do **not** treat those marks as actual worker responses without direct confirmation.

## Candidate onboarding value
This is highly valuable evidence for **worker preference and availability intake**. Candidate controlled fields include: contact method/preference, work interests, non-preferred work, duties/skills, schedule, availability, commute tolerance, broad work zones, compensation preferences, start timing, and job-value priorities.

It should augment—not replace—the existing applicant-intake evidence. A staged future flow can avoid asking all information at account creation and keep preference changes attributable/versioned.

## High-risk fields and non-negotiable review gates
The photographed form contains fields that must **not** become direct filters, employer-visible attributes, or automatic decision inputs without counsel/client policy approval:

1. **Country of origin** and named community experience/preference categories can implicate national-origin, ethnicity, religion, and related protected-characteristic concerns. Do not collect/use them for matching or display them to employers by default. Replace any actual job-relevant practice with narrowly defined, neutral skill/task requirements only after legal review.
2. **Kosher-home experience** may be a legitimate task-skill question only if expressed and used as a neutral, verified work capability—not as a religious affiliation/community filter. Counsel must validate wording, necessity, audience, and matching controls.
3. **Work authorization question** requires a separate employment-eligibility/I-9 workflow analysis. Do not make a blanket worker-intake gate, request documents, or expose it to employers from this form alone.
4. **Lifting, stairs, care, pets, and driving** can reveal or be used as disability/health proxies. Translate job needs into essential, specific task requirements; define accommodation/escalation and avoid automatic exclusion.
5. **Free-text responses** require content moderation, access controls, minimization, review cues, and retention policy. They must not bypass anti-discrimination/fair-chance rules.
6. **Pay preferences** are worker preferences, not employer job-ad pay ranges, fee calculations, or an authorization to use salary-history data.
7. **Location and travel** should use least-precise zones/travel tolerance; never expose worker home address or location inference to employers/public users.

## Product / governance implications
- Separate **worker self-reported preferences** from verified qualifications, employer requirements, agency review, and any counsel-approved match constraints.
- Any matching assistance must be transparent, human-reviewed, explainable, and prohibited from making autonomous employment decisions. Preference compatibility is not permission for opaque AI scoring.
- Preserve preference profile versions and changes with actor/time/source; preserve a snapshot used for each referral without locking a worker’s current profile to old data.
- Apply English/Spanish parity: the equivalent English experience needs approved, accessible content and identical functional/validation behavior. The form is evidence of Spanish-first operations, not an approved legal translation.
- Establish Google Form governance before continued use or migration: account ownership, access roles, response export/storage, retention/deletion, vendor/data processing, respondent notices, and migration provenance.

## Validation questions
1. Is this sent to workers/applicants, employers, or both? Who owns the Google account and can access/export responses?
2. Which fields are actually required, optional, used for review, visible to an employer, or used for reporting only?
3. Are `country of origin`, community labels, kosher experience, work authorization, lifting/stairs, and free text currently used in matching or screening? If yes, pause that use for counsel review.
4. What are the intended definitions for salary (hourly/weekly/annual; gross/net; live-in compensation), work zones, travel, and “any available work”?
5. What job-specific neutral skills/requirements should replace protected-characteristic/community questions?
6. Which aggregate metrics are genuinely needed, who can view them, and what minimum group size/privacy rules apply?
